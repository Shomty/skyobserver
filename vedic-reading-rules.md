# Rules for Reading a Personal Insight from a Vedic Rasi Chart

A working framework for turning D1/D9 chart data, Sudarshana Chakra, and Vimshottari Dasha timing into a personal insight reading. Each section names the chart or factor used, the data points to pull, and how to interpret them. Career (D10 Dashamsha) is covered separately and is out of scope here.

## 1. Core Identity and Personality (D1 Rashi Chart) — the Personality Wheel

The D1 chart maps the physical body and baseline karmas: the tangible, present-life personality.

### Step 1: Ascendant (Lagna) and Lagna Lord

- Note the element (Fire, Earth, Air, Water) and guna (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas) of the ascending sign. This sets baseline temperament and behavior.
- Locate the house and sign of the Lagna Lord. Its placement colors the whole personality: a Lagna Lord in the 10th house orients the person around career, public duty, and status; in the 4th, around home and emotional security; and so on for each house.

### Step 2: Moon (mind) and Sun (soul)

- Moon: note sign, house, and Nakshatra. This governs emotional needs, mental processing, and instinctual reactions.
- Sun: note sign and house. This governs ego, soul purpose, authority, and vital force.
- Synthesize all three: Lagna (outer physical self), Moon (inner emotional self), Sun (core vitality). The reading should describe how these three layers agree or pull against each other. This Lagna–Moon–Sun triad is the "Personality Wheel": the outward presentation, the inner emotional world, and the driving vitality underneath both.

### Step 3: Strengths and blind spots

- Strengths: flag any planet that is Exalted (Uchcha), in its Own Sign (Swakshetra), or in Mooltrikona.
- Blind spots: flag any planet that is Debilitated (Neecha), Combust, or Retrograde, especially if placed in a Dusthana house (6th, 8th, or 12th).

## 2. Inner Path and Marital Destiny (D9 Navamsha Chart)

D9 is the "fruit of the tree": D1 shows the promise, D9 shows what actually manifests, particularly in the second half of life, plus inner strength and marital dynamics.

### Step 1: Check for Vargottama planets

- Compare each planet's sign in D1 against its sign in D9.
- A planet in the identical sign in both charts is Vargottama, giving it extraordinary strength, resilience, and unshakeable dignity. Call these out explicitly.

### Step 2: Assess inner strength vs. outer promise

- A planet weak in D1 (e.g., debilitated) but exalted or strong in D9 suggests someone who overcomes early-life struggles to build deep internal resilience later.
- A planet strong in D1 but weak or debilitated in D9 suggests strong early promise that struggles to produce lasting results.
- This D1-vs-D9 comparison is the primary tool for reading inner self vs. outer self: D1 is what is visible and promised on the surface, D9 is what is actually true underneath and what endures.

### Step 3: Relationship axis

- Locate the Upapada Lagna (UL): count the distance from the 12th house lord to the 12th house.
- Benefics (Jupiter, Venus, Mercury) in or aspecting the UL indicate harmony, support, and an auspicious partner.
- Malefics (Saturn, Mars, Rahu, Ketu) influencing the UL indicate tests, delays, or structural friction in marriage.
- Cross-check the 7th house of D9 and its lord to confirm the spouse's core nature and long-term compatibility.

## 3. Life Mission (Dharma, Atmakaraka, Rahu–Ketu Axis)

This section is not in the original source text; it is added here because "life mission" cannot be answered by D1/D9/Dasha alone. It draws on standard Jaimini and Parashari conventions used widely in Vedic astrology. Treat the interpretive claims below as traditional convention, not verified fact.

### Step 1: Atmakaraka (AK)

- Identify the Atmakaraka: the planet with the highest degree within its sign among the seven classical grahas (Sun through Saturn), excluding Rahu and Ketu.
- The AK is considered the soul-significator. Its sign, house, and dignity in D1 (and D9) point to the core lesson or drive the person is here to work through.

### Step 2: 9th house (Dharma house)

- Note the sign on the 9th house, its lord's placement, and any occupying planets.
- The 9th house governs higher purpose, belief systems, teachers/guidance, and fortune. A strong, well-aspected 9th house and lord suggest a person who finds meaning through a clear philosophy, teacher, or calling.

### Step 3: Rahu–Ketu axis

- Note the house and sign placement of Rahu and Ketu (they are always exactly opposite one another).
- Common convention: Rahu marks the direction of unfamiliar growth and worldly desire the person is pulled toward in this life; Ketu marks what is already familiar or over-developed, often from past patterning, and what is meant to be released or transcended.
- Read the Rahu house as "what this life is pulling you to build," and the Ketu house as "what you already know how to do and may be over-relying on."

### Synthesis

- Combine AK sign/house, 9th house condition, and the Rahu house to describe the life mission as a direction rather than a fixed job title: what the person is drawn to grow into, and through what themes (house significations) that growth is likely to happen.

## 4. Shadow Work (Dusthanas, Saturn, Rahu–Ketu, Gulika/Mandi)

Also not in the original source text; added because "shadow work" needs its own dedicated read rather than being folded into a single blind-spots bullet.

### Step 1: Dusthana houses (6th, 8th, 12th)

- 6th house: conflict, debt, illness, daily struggle, and the patterns a person must actively manage rather than avoid.
- 8th house: hidden material, transformation, crisis, and what surfaces only under pressure.
- 12th house: loss, isolation, subconscious material, and unresolved or unseen patterns.
- Any Lagna Lord, Moon, Sun, or AK placed in or heavily aspected from a dusthana signals shadow material tied to that planet's themes.

### Step 2: Saturn

- Saturn's sign, house, and aspects mark where fear, restriction, self-doubt, or avoidance tend to show up, and also where discipline eventually produces mastery once faced directly.

### Step 3: Rahu–Ketu revisited

- Where Rahu and Ketu sit is also where shadow material concentrates: Rahu's house can bring compulsive or anxious over-reaching, Ketu's house can bring detachment, avoidance, or a blind spot the person assumes is already "handled."

### Step 4: Gulika and Mandi

- Locate Gulika (Gk) and Mandi (Md), the shadow/upagraha points.
- Their placement, and any affliction they cause to key houses (1st, 7th, 9th) in D1 or D9, marks specific karmic knots: areas where effort does not immediately show visible results, and where patience and inner work matter more than outward striving.

## 5. Sudarshana Chakra (Three-Ring Life-Area Timing)

The Sudarshana Chakra evaluates the same 12 houses from three different reference points at once: the Lagna (body/physical life), the Moon (mind/emotions), and the Sun (soul/authority). This triangulation is a direct tool for reading inner vs. outer self and for breaking life into areas, because it re-derives every house meaning from three vantage points simultaneously.

### Step 1: Build the three rings

- Lagna Chakra (inner layer): houses counted from the natal Ascendant sign. Governs physical life, body, and personal, day-to-day experience.
- Chandra Chakra (middle layer): houses counted from the natal Moon sign. Governs mind, emotions, and mental state.
- Surya Chakra (outer layer): houses counted from the natal Sun sign. Governs soul, authority, power, and father/public-facing identity.
- Each ring re-maps all 12 houses starting from its own reference sign, so a given planet can land in a different house number in each ring.

### Step 2: Triangulate the key life-area houses

For each of the following, read the same house across all three rings together rather than one ring at a time:

- 1st house (self, health, identity) across Lagna, Chandra, and Surya.
- 7th house (relationships, union) across all three rings.
- 2nd and 11th houses (wealth and inflow) across all three rings.
- (Career/10th-house triangulation is handled in the separate career reading and is out of scope here.)

Agreement across all three rings (e.g., benefics supporting the same house in all three) marks a genuine strength. Affliction repeating across all three rings marks a real, load-bearing blind spot rather than a minor one.

### Step 3: Active Sudarshana year

- Active house = (current age mod 12) + 1; if the remainder is 0, the active house is 12.
- Read this active house across all three rings (occupants, lord, aspects) to describe the theme of the current year specifically, distinct from the person's lifelong baseline.

## 7. Timing and Predictive Unfolding (Vimshottari Dasha)

The Dasha system maps when specific natal promises manifest across a 120-year cycle.

- Identify the active Mahadasha (major period). It sets the overarching life theme, e.g., a Jupiter Mahadasha shifts focus toward learning, wisdom, children, and expansion.
- Identify the active Antardasha (sub-period). It triggers specific events within the major theme, e.g., a Jupiter Mahadasha / Venus Antardasha can trigger marriage or financial progress if the placements are favorable.
- Cross-reference house lordships: check which houses the active Dasha lords rule in D1 and D9 to pinpoint the exact life area being activated right now (use the house table in Section 6 to name the area).

## 8. Practical Psychological Guidance Prompt (Gemini, secular — no Vedic terms in output)

Sections 1–7 produce astrological findings. This section turns those findings into practical, day-to-day coping guidance in plain psychological language, with zero astrology terminology in the output (no "chart," "planet," "house," "dasha," "Sanskrit terms," etc.).

### How to prepare the input

Before calling this prompt, translate the chart findings from Sections 1–6 into plain-language psychological themes — do this translation yourself, do not pass raw chart data into this prompt:

- Temperament: describe the D1 Lagna/Moon/Sun read (Section 1) as a plain-language temperament summary (e.g., "quick to act, emotionally guarded, driven by a need for recognition") instead of sign/planet names.
- Core strengths: describe the dignified/Vargottama placements (Sections 1 and 2) as behavioral strengths (e.g., "natural steadiness under pressure," "strong verbal persuasion").
- Growth edges: describe the debilitated/afflicted placements and dusthana involvement (Sections 1 and 4) as behavioral patterns (e.g., "tendency to avoid conflict until it boils over," "self-critical inner voice").
- Life direction: describe the Atmakaraka/9th-house/Rahu themes (Section 3) as a plain-language sense of direction (e.g., "drawn to lead and teach, still building confidence to claim that role").
- Current chapter: describe the active Dasha and active Sudarshana house (Sections 5 and 7) as "what this period of life is asking of them" in plain terms (e.g., "a stretch of life centered on career visibility and public recognition").

### The prompt template

```
System/Role Prompt:
You are a grounded, secular personal-development guide. You draw on established, evidence-informed
frameworks — cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), Jungian
shadow-work concepts (used in their general psychological sense, not astrological), attachment theory,
and strengths-based coaching. You do not use astrology, Vedic terminology, planet names, house numbers,
Sanskrit words, or any reference to charts or destiny. Speak the way a thoughtful, well-read coach or
counselor would speak to a client: plain, warm, direct, and practical.

Task Context:
You will receive a plain-language psychological profile for a person (temperament, core strengths, growth
edges, life direction, and current chapter of life — already translated out of any astrological source).
Your job is to turn this into practical coping and growth guidance for daily life. Do not diagnose. Do not
claim clinical authority. This is coaching-style guidance, not therapy.

Native profile (plain language, no astrology terms):
- Temperament: {temperament}
- Core strengths: {core_strengths}
- Growth edges / patterns to watch: {growth_edges}
- Life direction: {life_direction}
- Current chapter: {current_chapter}

Guidelines:
1. Ground every suggestion in the specific profile above — no generic self-help filler.
2. Offer concrete, doable practices (a specific action, question, or exercise), not vague encouragement.
3. Name the underlying pattern plainly before offering the practice, so the person recognizes themselves.
4. Where the growth edges resemble something that could be a real mental health concern (e.g., persistent
   low mood, anxiety that disrupts daily function, patterns of self-harm), say plainly that this is worth
   discussing with a licensed therapist or counselor, and do not attempt to treat it yourself.
5. Never suggest physical pain, extreme restriction, or discomfort as a coping mechanism.
6. Write in second person ("you"), 2–4 short paragraphs per field, plain prose (no bullet lists inside
   fields unless the field specifically calls for steps).

Return JSON with these fields:
1. selfUnderstanding — how the temperament and core strengths show up in daily behavior, framed as
   self-awareness rather than judgment.
2. copingStrategies — specific, practical coping strategies for the named growth edges (e.g., a concrete
   CBT-style reframe, an ACT-style acceptance practice, a boundary script for a real recurring situation).
3. dailyPractices — 2–3 small, concrete daily or weekly practices the person can start this week.
4. currentChapterGuidance — practical advice for navigating what the "current chapter" section describes,
   in terms of decisions, relationships, or habits right now.
5. whenToSeekSupport — plain, non-alarming guidance on the kinds of signs that suggest it's time to loop in
   a therapist, counselor, or doctor, and how to bring that up with themselves or others.
```

### Output contract (mirrors the existing app pattern)

Match the JSON-schema pattern already used elsewhere in the app (see `generateSudarshanaChakraInterpretation` in `src/services/geminiService.ts`): a `Type.OBJECT` schema with the five string fields above, all required, parsed with a try/catch that throws a clear "Invalid AI response format" error on failure, and passed through `callGeminiProxy` — never instantiate `GoogleGenAI` directly, and never let this call bypass the existing 429/5xx retry wrapper.

### Guardrails specific to this prompt

- No astrology leakage: if the model's output contains words like "planet," "house," "chart," "dasha," "rashi," or any Sanskrit term, treat that as a failed generation and regenerate — the translation step above exists specifically to prevent this.
- No fabricated clinical claims: the prompt must not have the model cite specific studies, statistics, or named clinical trials. General framework names (CBT, ACT) are fine to reference; invented citations are not.
- No self-harm coping techniques: per general safety practice, do not let this prompt suggest physical discomfort (ice, pain, restriction) as an emotional regulation tool.
- This is coaching content, not therapy or diagnosis. The output should say so if asked, and should route toward professional support for anything beyond everyday coping.

## How the Readings Combine

The user's six target outcomes, and which sections feed each one:

- Personality Wheel: Section 1 (D1 Lagna, Moon, Sun triad).
- Inner vs. outer self: Section 2 (D1 vs. D9 comparison) plus Section 5 (Sudarshana's inner/middle/outer ring structure — Lagna=body, Chandra=mind, Surya=soul).
- Strengths and blind spots: Section 1, Step 3 (dignities and afflictions in D1) confirmed or amplified by Section 5, Step 2 (agreement or affliction repeating across all three Sudarshana rings).
- Life mission: Section 3 (Atmakaraka, 9th house, Rahu) — this was missing from the original framework and has been added.
- Shadow work: Section 4 (dusthanas, Saturn, Rahu–Ketu, Gulika/Mandi) — this was also missing and has been added as its own section rather than a single bullet.
- Life-area breakdowns: Section 6 (house significations table), applied through D1, D9, and the Sudarshana rings (Section 5), and dated by Dasha (Section 7).
- Practical, real-world coping advice: Section 8 — takes the plain-language translation of all of the above and turns it into secular, psychology-grounded guidance with no astrology terms in the output.

## Notes on using this framework

- This is an interpretive framework, not an astronomical or astrological ground truth. Treat it as a lens for organizing chart data into a narrative, not as a verified predictive method.
- Any specific claim in a generated reading (e.g., "AK in the 9th means X") is a traditional interpretive convention, not an empirically verified fact. Present it as astrological convention, not established fact.
- Sections 3 and 4 (Life Mission, Shadow Work) were not present in the original source material and were added to close the gap for those two requested outcomes; flag this if precision about provenance matters downstream.
